Timing diagram

One column is one gate delay (τ). A carry cannot settle until the carry before it has. A signal that flips more than once on its way to its final value is glitching. Click the diagram to move the playhead. The dashed line marks the 4-bit worst case.

What each stage does

A stage generates a carry when A and B are both 1, kills it when both are 0, and passes the incoming carry along when they differ. A long run of passing stages is what makes the adder slow.

Delay grows with width

Each extra stage puts one more AND and one more OR on the carry path. A carry-lookahead adder works out the carries in parallel groups instead.

Lookahead figures are idealized: 4-bit groups and gates with unlimited fan-in.

Brightness add in an image pipeline

Brightening an image adds a constant to every pixel. Here A is a 4-bit grayscale pixel and B is the brightness boost. The carry out of the last stage, C4, tells the pipeline that the result overflowed.

Model: every gate has the same delay τ, wires add no delay, and each stage is two XOR gates, two AND gates and one OR gate. Edit τ above to convert gate delays to nanoseconds.
